SENTINEL / OPTICAL INSTRUMENT
Implementation brief and reusable authoring prompt

Build the existing Sentinel administrator login and observability dashboard around an original, machined optical instrument using Three.js. Keep the application's Korean typography, working authentication and live telemetry. The latest user feedback calls for convincing material, weight and light instead of a decorative stack of shiny rings.

Art direction
Use a circular titanium chassis and thin, skeletonized bridges around a recessed optical cartridge. Mill real through-pockets into the bridges; expose the bearing races, balls, hub gears and lower ribs between layers. Use recessed hex sockets with visible depth and keep guides aligned with their seats. Let broad neutral studio reflections reveal the satin metal, with brighter polished bevels and darker coated barrels. Separate broad bridge faces with black lands and narrow polished shoulders. Filter fine directional tooling marks at distance. Distinguish coated optical glass, rubber seals, warm nickel internals and steel guide rods through their materials. Use a recessed cold-white aperture, restrained reflected spill and deep shadows between the layers. Avoid gold trim, decorative background rings and floating HUD decoration. Neutral typography and a quiet login form sit beside the instrument.

Physical setting
Treat the background as a real studio set, not an empty black surface. Place the assembly on a ball seat over a layered, chamfered metal deck with fasteners, isolation feet and a discreet physical maker's plate. Use graphite wall panels, recessed joints, one fluted section and a narrow built-in diffuser behind the subject. The deck and floor establish perspective; broad surface reflections connect the instrument with its surroundings. Keep text clear of the practical light. Let the fixed set recede slightly out of focus and use a wider camera to show the base. Batch fixed geometry by finish to keep the new setting affordable on mobile; preserve object-space machining on turned fasteners.

Geometry and motion
A fixed rear housing supports a guided split cover, optical cartridge and independent lens. Unlock before lifting; separate the cover, carriage and optic in sequence. Keep guides attached and stop rods inside their seats. The iris opens gently. Closing follows the same path in reverse. Retain the damped master travel so reversing mid-travel preserves velocity. Use held endpoints and no camera jump at loop boundaries. Automatic, exploded and assembled controls plus a persistent motion toggle remain available.

Lighting and camera
Use the self-hosted CC0 photographic HDR environment and large rectangular softboxes for continuous metal highlights. One directional key supplies shadows because Three.js rectangular lights do not cast them. Depth contact shading clarifies gaps. Treat the scene as clear studio air: show light on the surfaces, without an added cone or broad atmospheric halo. A weak area diffuser under the optic illuminates the carriage; avoid point lights almost touching the polished rim. Retain only a narrow optical fringe around very bright edges. Average the source pixels before blur and bound the extracted bloom energy so isolated specular peaks cannot expand into white disks. Keep the floor dark with a broad, restrained reflection and no mirror effect. Use a low oblique camera that shows housing thickness, a small pointer response and restrained focus falloff. Do not claim browser rasterization has achieved offline film quality.

Engineering constraints
Self-host pinned Three.js and its HDR/LTC helpers with source and license notices. Preserve CSP and the exact public-file allowlist. Authentication and monitoring must work without WebGL. The fallback poster is captured from the actual current geometry, not the earlier concept artwork. Suspend animation when hidden or offscreen and honor reduced motion. Cap pixel density and frame rate, reduce mobile samples and omit glass transmission there. Dispose scene resources and all postprocessing render targets on navigation. Never present the decorative animation as infrastructure telemetry.

Startup and light cue
Keep the login form usable from the first paint. While modules, geometry, lighting and GPU programs prepare, show a small engraved outline over the darkened matching poster with four actual preparation stages. Do not display a fabricated percentage or add a minimum wait. Fetch the HDR alongside the module graph, share the machining texture between the instrument and set, and yield between geometry construction steps. After the first GPU frame, a single rim light travels across the shoulders, the cold-white aperture wakes, and a broad neutral key with a quiet champagne bounce reveals the metal. A small camera move settles over 3.8 seconds and hands over to the mechanical cycle. Avoid a broad glow or flashing effect. Reduced motion and a saved pause show the fully lit static composition. Graphics failure restores the full poster and leaves authentication available.

Transfer budget
Compress the exact public JavaScript, CSS and HDR paths at the existing proxy. Allow public visual files to reuse bytes only after ETag revalidation, because their paths are not content-hashed. Keep HTML, authentication, telemetry, private resources and failures under no-store. Verify both rules through the real proxy and measure public asset bytes after deployment; do not equate wire-byte reduction with guaranteed GPU startup speed.

Acceptance
Inspect assembled and exploded states at 1280x720, 390x844 and a tablet breakpoint. Verify changing frames, pause/resume, page transitions, no horizontal overflow, fallback loading and real server telemetry. Ship through the existing PR and automated deployment with actual screenshots and test evidence.
